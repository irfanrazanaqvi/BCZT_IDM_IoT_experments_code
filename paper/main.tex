\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Industrial plants run long-lived devices, legacy equipment that cannot hold cryptographic keys, and assets shared by several organizations. Identity systems built on a single certificate authority, session tokens or periodically refreshed revocation lists fit this setting poorly: a revoked device keeps working until a token expires or a list is refreshed, no single party is trusted by all to revoke it, and one trust threshold serves every request. We present a blockchain-enabled Zero Trust architecture for industrial IoT identity management. Three smart contracts on a permissioned proof-of-authority ledger hold device identity and status, a tiered access policy with an anomaly gate and a strict mode for safety-relevant writes, and quorum-voting revocation. Zone gateways decide routine requests locally against replicated ledger state, high-consequence requests are decided on-chain, and batch revocation supports firmware recalls. We prove immediate revocation on the on-chain path, a bounded exposure window on the fast path, replay resistance, attestation unforgeability, key-reuse prevention and governance safety. In simulation the on-chain path accepted no request after revocation, against 33--147 for token and list baselines. We then replace the synthetic behavioural signal with real traffic from three datasets (TON\_IoT, WUSTL-IIOT-2018 and WUSTL-IIoT-2021). The full policy accepted under 1\% of attack requests of seen types at the critical tiers, but 6.6--11.9\% of held-out types and 29--77\% when an adaptive attacker sent only its best-scoring flows. The outcome depends on the detector: a supervised detector accepted 75--78\% of withheld attack types at the upper tiers of one dataset, whereas a detector trained on normal traffic blocked them at the price of denying 12--22\% of legitimate requests at the two critical tiers. On a four-validator Hyperledger Besu network, on one host and with validators in four Google Cloud regions, audited calls were final in about one 2\,s block, and across regions a revocation was visible on a remote validator 1.8\,s after submission. These are single runs without injected delay or partitions, and posture and network signals remain synthetic. Code is released at \href{https://github.com/irfanrazanaqvi/BCZT_IDM_IoT_experments_code}{github.com/irfanrazanaqvi/BCZT\_IDM\_IoT\_experments\_code}.

\noindent\textbf{Index Terms}\textemdash Industrial IoT, Zero Trust Architecture, Blockchain, Decentralized Identifiers, Identity Management, Smart Contracts, Revocation Governance, Operational Technology.
\end{abstract}
